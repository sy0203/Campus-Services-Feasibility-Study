## Overview

A student's day can involve checking a course portal, presenting a student card, unlocking a residence building and paying with a meal plan. Each activity may depend on a different account, application or physical credential.

**UNIFIED proposes bringing these interactions into one mobile platform.** Our team examined the product's potential value, the challenges of university adoption, the resources needed to operate the service and the financial assumptions behind a possible launch.

The project connects **product strategy, market research, competitive analysis and financial modelling** in a single business case. Its main deliverable is the [complete feasibility report](Business_Feasibility_Report.pdf).

## Proposed Platform

UNIFIED is designed around a shared interface for students, with universities and colleges as institutional customers.

| Service area | Proposed capability | Intended benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Academics** | Course registration, schedules, grades and course information | Keep essential academic information accessible in one place. |
| **Student identification** | Digital student ID and campus credentials | Reduce reliance on separate physical cards. |
| **Residence and building access** | Smartphone access through compatible NFC and access-control systems | Make campus access available through a student's phone. |
| **Campus payments** | Meal-plan balances, payments and transfers between eligible campus accounts | Simplify access to campus funds and services. |
| **Student services** | Residence applications and student profile information | Reduce navigation between separate administrative platforms. |

These are proposed capabilities. The repository documents the feasibility study; it does not contain a deployed application or implementation code.

## Feasibility Analysis

The report evaluates the concept through four areas.

### 1. Product feasibility

- Defined the student needs and campus activities the platform would support.
- Examined existing cloud services, NFC payments and smartphone access-control systems as reference points for technical feasibility.
- Considered customization for universities with different service and infrastructure requirements.

### 2. Industry feasibility

- Applied **Porter's Five Forces** to assess competition, entry barriers, substitutes, and buyer and supplier power.
- Compared the proposed service with existing university applications and physical access systems.
- Examined switching costs, implementation requirements and universities' ability to develop their own solutions.

### 3. Organizational feasibility

- Outlined responsibilities across management, technology, marketing, finance and human resources.
- Identified additional product, risk-management and legal capabilities that could be needed as the venture expanded.
- Considered staffing, office space, equipment and customer-support requirements.

### 4. Financial feasibility

- Estimated startup funding and a proposed mix of equity and debt financing.
- Linked per-student pricing and enrolment scenarios to projected revenue.
- Prepared a five-year income forecast and a projected first-year balance sheet.
- Estimated development, maintenance, support, payroll, office and financing costs.

## Business Model

The proposal assumes an **annual fee of CAD $10 per student**, with adoption arranged through participating institutions. The rollout scenario starts with Toronto-area campuses before expanding to other Ontario universities.

Revenue is tied directly to the assumed student population covered by the service. For example, the first-year scenario uses:

**61,690 students × CAD $10 annually = CAD $616,900 in projected revenue.**

The 61,690 figure is a planning input, not a count of acquired users. Institutional adoption and the cost of connecting existing campus systems remain central assumptions in the business case.

### Five-year forecast

The figures below reproduce the report's **2023–2027 planning scenario**. They represent projections, not actual revenue, funding raised or operating results.

| Forecast year | Revenue (CAD) | Net income / loss (CAD) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 2023 | $616,900.00 | -$179,793.17 |
| 2024 | $980,370.00 | $207,966.63 |
| 2025 | $1,510,370.00 | $430,400.93 |
| 2026 | $1,842,360.00 | $374,362.48 |
| 2027 | $2,159,360.00 | $447,786.10 |

Under the model's assumptions, annual net income becomes positive in 2024. However, projected profit declines in 2026 despite higher revenue, alongside increases in customer-support and maintenance expenses. This illustrates how the cost of serving more institutions can affect profitability.

## Key Takeaways

- **Student convenience and institutional value must align.** A simpler student experience needs to justify the university's integration effort and switching costs.
- **Technical feasibility is one part of adoption.** Existing payment and access-control technologies provide reference points, while compatibility with each institution's systems requires further assessment.
- **Growth changes the operating model.** Expansion brings additional support, maintenance and staffing needs that must be reflected in financial planning.

## Scope and Next Steps

This project is a team feasibility study. Its research, pricing and cost assumptions reflect the report's original planning context, including pandemic-era campus requirements. The financial model would need updated inputs and reconciliation before further business use.

Potential next steps include structured interviews with students and university administrators, assessment of integration and access-control requirements, financial sensitivity analysis, and a limited campus pilot. These are proposed extensions to the study.
